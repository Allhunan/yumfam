Shanghai ICE FAMILY Ice Park ¥61 (≈$8.50) for 2 Sessions: 90-Minute Skates at LaLaport Jinqiao

Shanghai ICE FAMILY ice park at ¥61 (≈$8.50, was ¥200): a single-person 2-visit card for 90-minute sessions each on the real-ice rink at LaLaport Jinqiao — use it twice yourself or bring a friend. Skates, helmet and full protective gear included; staff help with fitting. Valid to Dec 31, 2026, weekends and holidays included.

Deal price: ¥61 (≈$8.50, was ¥200) · ⏰ Valid May 27 – Dec 31, 2026 · ⛸️ 2 × 90-minute sessions · Skates, helmet & protective gear included · Refundable anytime

At roughly ¥30 per session — about 30% of the walk-in price — this is Shanghai’s cheapest ticket onto real ice. ICE FAMILY’s rink at LaLaport Jinqiao (Pudong) is the trendy, beginner-friendly one: a fashion-forward space with a huge real-ice floor, 90 minutes of skating per visit, and full kit provided — skates, helmet and protective gear for head, knees, elbows and feet. Staff help you gear up before you step on and walk you through the on-ice basics.

The 2-visit card is flexible: use it twice yourself across two visits, or take two people in a single visit — adults and kids alike, one card covers any combination. Beginners are genuinely welcome: the rink stocks cute Bobby/Tommy skating aids (animal-shaped push supports that let total first-timers glide with confidence — rental is extra), and the ice is resurfaced daily with skates re-sharpened for a good glide. Gloves aren’t included and cost ¥20 on site — bring your own if you have cut-resistant ones.

⚠️ Fine print that matters: usable Monday to Sunday, including weekends and holidays. Refundable anytime before use. Buy and walk in — no ticket exchange needed. The Bobby skating aids are not included in the ticket price.

Packages

  • Single-person 2-visit card (90 min per visit; use as 1 person × 2 or 2 people × 1), gear included — ¥61 (≈$8.50, was ¥200)

Good to know: a “valid through year-end, any day including holidays” card at this price is genuinely rare — ice-rink cards usually black out weekends. If your kid has been begging to try skating, this is the low-stakes way in.
