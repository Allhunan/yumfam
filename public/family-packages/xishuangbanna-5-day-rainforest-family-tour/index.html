Winter in Xishuangbanna is the opposite of winter almost everywhere else in China: warm, green, and smelling of rain. This 5-day tour is built around a 2,930-mu private rainforest on Jino Mountain — a patch of forest the operator spent two years scouting — where indigenous Jino guides lead the hike, teaching kids to recognise strangler figs, buttress roots, century-old dragon-scale trees and the ancient fern Alsophila. The pace is deliberately unhurried: no herding, no crowds, and the group caps at 25 people.

The five days mix rainforest, science and Dai culture. In the private forest there’s a rainforest feast served in the wild, stream play — water fights, catching crabs and scooping up small fish — face-painting with coloured stones, an off-road drive through the jungle, a visit to the insect museum (300+ specimens, 200 species) where kids make their own insect specimen to take home, and a bean-pod doll craft session followed by sunset from the camp viewpoint. The classic sights are covered too: the Chinese Academy of Sciences botanical garden (11,250 hectares, 13,000+ living plant species), Wild Elephant Valley — feed an elephant, visit the Asian Elephant Museum, walk the canopy boardwalk — the Dai Ethnic Garden’s thousand-year-old village and its 1,400-year-old temple, a water-splashing party with the full traditional ritual, slow-wheel pottery (a fully handmade, no-machinery craft), and the “Meet the Dai Princess” immersive role-play through Manting Park, where the family follows NPCs through the old Dai royal garden.

The logistics are kind to families: 4 nights in the same 5-diamond hotel (Xinshiji Rainforest Time, or an equivalent) — no repacking, no changing hotels — in the Manongfeng resort area, a short drive from the airport and high-speed rail station, with a kids’ playground, an adult pool and a children’s splash area. The price covers the coach, the 4 hotel nights, breakfast on days 2–5 plus the marked lunches and dinners (including the rainforest feast and the elephant meal), all first-gate tickets, the listed experiences, a family tour leader, a local guide, hats, backpacks and a study handbook, a souvenir certificate, drinking water, travel insurance, and a photographer when the group reaches 10 people.

Good to know: this is a destination-joining tour — flights and trains to and from Xishuangbanna are not included, so buy your transport only after customer service confirms the group is running. One family can form a group. Ages are open, but pregnant travellers cannot join, and families with travellers over 65 (or over 70, or with heart, respiratory or similar conditions) should tell the operator before booking. Pack spring/autumn clothes plus a light jacket, rain boots for adults and kids over 1.35 m, water shoes for younger kids, sunscreen and sturdy walking shoes. The operator asks families who want a nanny-style service to think twice — this is an outdoor adventure trip, and the itinerary can shift with weather or local festivals.
