# China Hot Springs with Kids: Where to Soak This Winter

Winter is hot-spring season in China, and for families it works in a way first-time visitors rarely expect: the spring water is often piped straight into your room. Instead of queuing for a public bath with a toddler in tow, you can soak in a private pool on your own balcony, then wander down to a garden of shared pools when the kids want to explore. The deals below are all verified on sale now, and they show the three shapes a family hot-spring stay can take — a room with its own pool, a whole villa, or a water town where the hot spring is one part of a bigger day.

## The three shapes, and which fits your family

**A room with a private pool** is the easiest first try. You get the soak without the logistics: no carrying towels and swim nappies across a resort, no watching the clock on a public pool's opening hours. **A whole villa** suits a big family or two families travelling together — one booking, several bedrooms, one pool you do not share with strangers. **A hot spring inside a bigger trip** makes sense when the kids would get bored of soaking all day: the spring is the evening wind-down, and the daytime is a town, a wall, or a water park.

## 1. A private pool in your room: Changtai Banyue Mountain, Fujian

Banyue Mountain sits in a forested valley in Changtai, about an hour from Xiamen, built around strontium-rich soda springs and a hot-spring park with 50+ pools. The deal is a 50-square-metre mansion room with its own private hot-spring soak pool, plus breakfast and unlimited park access for 2 adults and 2 kids (one under 1.4 m, one under 1.2 m), from ¥799 (about $111, was ¥1,398), valid through December 30, 2026.

That private pool is the point. You can bathe after the kids are asleep, or let a small child paddle in warm water while you stay in the room with them. When you do want the park, the 50+ pools are unlimited during your stay, and the park runs 13:00–23:00. Saturdays add ¥100 a night. Children 1.2–1.4 m pay ¥88 for the hot spring and ¥68 for breakfast on site; over 1.4 m pays the adult rate of ¥199. A shuttle runs from Xiamen North Station if you book it a day ahead, and you book the room by 23:59 the day before — unbooked orders are refundable.

Families rate it highly: 92.3% of families with kids aged 3–6 recommend it, 90.5% for ages 7–9 and 91.7% for ages 10–12, across 144 merchant reviews. One guest called it "still my favourite place for hot springs and swimming — the best choice for a weekend mini-break!"

## 2. A whole villa to yourselves: Huizhou Fuli Yangshenggu, Guangdong

Two hours from Guangzhou or Shenzhen, in the Nankun Mountain area, Fuli Yangshenggu offers a whole 4-bedroom villa with its own private hot-spring soak pool from ¥489 (about $68, was ¥1,288), valid November 18, 2026 to January 31, 2027. A 5-bedroom villa is ¥666 and a 6-bedroom ¥788. The villa comes with free KTV, an electric barbecue grill, a kitchen with utensils and an automatic mahjong table — it is a house for the weekend, not a hotel room.

Sunday to Friday carries no surcharge, which is the real saving here: Saturdays add ¥300 a night in November and ¥500 in December and January, and New Year (January 1–2, 2027) adds ¥800. Two things to know before you book. First, breakfast and meals are not included — bring your own food for the barbecue and kitchen. Second, this villa product is newly listed, so the booking platform has no guest reviews for it yet; Trip.com rates the wider Fuli Nankun Mountain Hot Springs Valley resort area 4.3 out of 5 from 508 reviews, and one guest there called it "a lot of fun — clean water, friendly staff, very comfortable." The hot spring runs 14:00–22:30, and you can book by 16:00 on the day itself.

## 3. The balcony pool: Yongchun Tangcheng, Quanzhou

Tangcheng Yupin Hot Spring Hotel in Yongchun, Quanzhou — about 15 minutes from the train station — pipes spring water into the rooms themselves. Every room in this deal comes with its own balcony hot-spring pool, plus breakfast for 2 adults and 2 kids (under 1.2 m) and unlimited access to a hot-spring park with 20+ garden pools, from ¥428 (about $59, was ¥738), valid through December 31, 2026.

The park is the playful one: flower-petal pools, a mahjong pool, private nooks, plus an infinity pool and a kids' water park that takes over in the afternoons. Friday and Saturday stays add ¥100 per package, and children 1.2–1.4 m pay top-ups on site (breakfast ¥39, hot-spring ticket ¥49 on weekdays). Nearby day trips include 4A-rated Jiuxian Mountain and Baizhang Rock if one soak is enough and the kids want a walk.

## 4. Hot spring as part of a bigger day: Gubei Water Town, Beijing

If your family would not happily spend a whole day soaking, Gubei Water Town bundles the spring into a fuller trip. One night in a canal-side inn from ¥399 (about $55, was ¥510), valid November 20, 2026 to July 15, 2027, includes scenic-area tickets for two, hot-spring bath tickets for two, and a choice of one extra: Simatai Great Wall daytime tickets for two, a DIY lamp, or afternoon tea for two.

The town is at its best after dark, when the canals light up and the drone show runs over the rooftops, and the kids' play hall, pools and hot springs fill the daytime. Saturdays add ¥100 a night (¥200 on Fridays and Saturdays in July 2027), and Spring Festival (February 5–11, 2027) carries surcharges of ¥100–¥300 a night. Families rate it highly too: 91.7% of families with kids 3–6 recommend it, 93.8% for ages 7–9 and 94.1% for ages 10–12, across 64 merchant reviews. One guest wrote: "the kids played for two whole days and loved the pool, the hot springs and the kids' play hall."

## The rules that catch first-timers out

- **Child height, not age, sets the price.** Nearly every deal above prices children by height — under 1.2 m, 1.2–1.4 m, over 1.4 m counts as an adult — and the thresholds and top-ups differ by property. Measure before you book, and expect to pay the top-up on site.
- **Weekends cost more, weekdays often do not.** Saturday surcharges run ¥100–¥500 a night on these deals, while several carry no surcharge Sunday to Friday. A Friday-night stay can cost noticeably more than a Sunday-night one for the same room.
- **"Private pool" has three meanings.** It can be a pool in your room (Changtai), a pool on your balcony (Yongchun), or a pool belonging to a whole villa (Fuli). All three are private, but only the villa gives you a pool no other guest's room shares — check which one the listing means.
- **Book the day before, and know the refund rule.** These deals are booked by 23:59 the day before (Fuli allows booking by 16:00 on the day), and unbooked orders are refundable — but once booked, the room is held for the full day and is not refundable. Do not book before your train or flight is certain.
- **Breakfast is not always included.** Changtai and Yongchun include it; Fuli's villa does not. If you are travelling with small children, that one line in the inclusions list matters more than the headline price.

## The bottom line

If you want the simplest possible first hot-spring trip, take a room with its own pool — Changtai from ¥799 or Yongchun from ¥428 — and let the shared park be optional. If you are travelling as a big family or with friends, the Fuli villa from ¥489 is a whole house for the price of a hotel room, as long as you go on a weekday and bring your own food. And if the kids will not sit in warm water all day, Gubei from ¥399 folds the soak into a water town, a Great Wall and a drone show.
