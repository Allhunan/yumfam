# China Hotel Buffets with Kids: What to Expect (and What to Pay)

A hotel buffet is one of the easiest first meals for a family visiting China. Nobody has to order in Chinese, nobody has to guess what a dish is, and a picky child can eat plain rice and watermelon while the adults work through the seafood counter. The catch is that the price on the door is rarely the price you pay: most five-star hotels in China sell the same buffet as a discounted deal ticket online, often 40–50% off the walk-in price, with the discount tied to a fixed validity window and a child policy that changes from hotel to hotel.

This guide explains how the system works, then lists four buffets you can actually book right now — two in Xiamen, two in Guangzhou — with the exact prices, dates and child rules from their deal pages.

## How a Chinese hotel buffet deal works

**You buy a ticket, not a table.** The deal is a voucher for a set package — lunch for one, dinner for two, two adults plus two kids — bought in advance and shown at the restaurant. Walk-in prices are higher; the ticket price is the real price. All four buffets below are sold this way.

**Lunch is the bargain.** Lunch costs roughly two-thirds of dinner, and the spread is close to the same — the dinner premium mostly buys a few extra stations (grilled oysters, teppanyaki, a plated course) and alcohol. If you are eating with kids, lunch is almost always the better deal.

**Child rules are per-hotel, not standard.** Some hotels go by age (under 6 free, 6–12 half price), others by height (under 1.2 m free, under 1.4 m on a child ticket). A "free child" usually means the buffet only — at the Conrad in Xiamen, for example, children eating free at dinner do not get the served plated course. Always check the rule on the deal page before you buy, and bring ID for your child: hotels do check height at the door.

**Book ahead, and mind the blackout dates.** Most of these tickets must be booked by the evening before (Kempinski: by 20:00 the day before; Wanfu Hilton: by noon on the day). Christmas Eve, New Year's Day and the Spring Festival week are common blackout dates. Unbooked tickets are generally refundable, and an expired unbooked voucher usually auto-refunds.

**Weekends are different.** Some buffets only run on weekends, some add a course or an activity on Saturday and Sunday, and a few charge the same price all week. Check the day of the week before you set your plans.

## 1. Xiamen Kempinski — lunch from ¥156 (≈$22)

The Kempinski is the 198-metre sail-shaped tower on Yundang Lake in central Xiamen, and its second-floor café has been serving its buffet for 12 years. Expect 100+ dishes: a fresh seafood counter, European, Japanese and Southeast Asian stations and Chinese classics, all cooked to order at open kitchens. Dinner adds salmon, grilled oysters, teppanyaki foie gras and Peking duck, served course by course.

- **Price:** lunch for one ¥156 (≈$22, was ¥278); lunch for two ¥306; dinner for one ¥218; dinner for two ¥428.
- **Valid:** November 25, 2026 – February 13, 2027, Wednesday to Sunday only. Blackout December 24, 2026 and February 5, 2027. Book by 20:00 the day before.
- **Kids:** every two adults can bring one child under 6 free; children 6–12 pay half the walk-in price, and children 12 and over pay the full adult price. Weekends and New Year carry no surcharge.

Guests praise the setting and the food — "unique decor, upscale and spacious, clean, fresh ingredients, lots of choice" — but one reviewer warns the steak queue can be slow at peak times, with no portioning, so go early if your child has their heart set on steak. ## 2. Xiamen Conrad — from ¥188 (≈$26), kids under 12 eat free

The Conrad sits in one of Xiamen's Twin Towers, and its buffet restaurant is on the 38th floor — the view is the point as much as the food, with the sea, Gulangyu Island and the Xiamen University campus spread out below the windows. Go at sunset if you can. The food leans on the classics done well — salmon sashimi that guests say never runs out, oysters and lamb, and a dessert counter guests single out — rather than sheer volume, and the room itself is on the small side.

- **Price:** single lunch plus one free child ¥188 (≈$26, was ¥308); double lunch plus two free children ¥358; single dinner plus one free child ¥238; double dinner plus two free children ¥458.
- **Valid:** November 19, 2026 – January 31, 2027. Dinner includes hairy crab until December 14; a winter hotpot joins the dinner spread from December 15 through January 31. Dinner is blacked out December 24, 25 and 31.
- **Kids:** each adult can bring one child under 12 free, so a family of two adults and two children eats on the double tier. Children eating free at dinner do not get the served plated course.

## 3. Guangzhou HNA Westin — "Fish & Crab Feast", 2 adults + 2 kids from ¥348 (≈$48)

The Westin's themed buffet is the most theatrical of the four. The headline upgrades land on weekends: Saturday–Sunday lunch guests each get a complimentary Buddha-Jumps-Over-the-Wall (abalone, dried scallops and sea cucumber in a sweet, rich broth), while weekend dinner adds a hairy crab per seat plus a live band. Weekend lunches also run kids' themed activities, and lunch comes with free-flow beer and soft drinks (dinner adds red and white wine).

- **Price:** 2 adults + 2 kids (kids 1.2 m / 1.4 m): lunch ¥348, dinner ¥498–¥518. 1 adult + 1 kid: lunch ¥168, dinner ¥258. Child-only ticket (under 1.4 m): lunch ¥108, dinner ¥138.
- **Valid:** to December 31, 2026 (the ¥498 dinner tier ends November 30). Usable even during the National Day holiday week, refundable anytime.
- **Kids:** the child ticket is height-based (under 1.4 m), not age-based — measure your child before you buy.

One regular writes of eating "half a catty of salmon" at lunch and calls the Buddha-Jumps-Over-the-Wall "wonderfully sweet and fresh". Another notes the garlic crayfish could have used more flavour, but "everything else was solid".

## 4. Guangzhou Wanfu Hilton — crab buffet, 1 adult + 1 child from ¥168 (≈$23)

The Wanfu Hilton in Baiyun district, right by the Guangzhou Children's Activity Center, has put a crab theme on its buffet for the autumn: "Golden Autumn with Crabs". The headline is the per-person crab dish — weekdays you get drunken hairy crab or steamed hairy crab with ginger vinegar; Friday to Sunday you get both. Around it, the buffet runs to 100+ dishes: a crab station (Singapore black-pepper crab, salted-egg-yolk crab, Typhoon Shelter crab, Thai yellow-curry crab), seafood, sashimi and sushi, grilled oysters and skewers, roast meats and Cantonese barbecue, and desserts including durian cake and eight flavours of ice cream.

- **Price:** 1 adult + 1 child lunch (weekends) ¥168 (≈$23, was ¥299); 2 adults + 2 kids lunch ¥299. Dinner: 1 adult + 1 child ¥248 Monday–Thursday, ¥318 any day; 2 adults + 2 kids ¥488 Monday–Thursday, ¥598 any day.
- **Valid:** lunch September 25 – December 27, 2026, weekends only; dinner packages to December 31, 2026. Book by noon on the day of use.
- **Kids:** the lunch child ticket is for a child under 1.2 m; the double lunch covers one child under 1.4 m and one under 1.2 m. Weekend lunch includes a kids' cooking class — DIY cakes, burgers and other hands-on food.

Guests call it "great value for money" and note the location by the Children's Activity Center makes it "perfect for a family outing".

## Tips for families

**Pick lunch.** Same hotel, same room, most of the same food, a third less money — and kids are fresher at noon than at 19:00.

**Check the child rule twice.** Age-based and height-based rules are both in use, and the cut-offs (6 vs 12 years, 1.2 m vs 1.4 m) decide whether your child eats free, half price or full price. The difference for a family of four can be ¥200 or more.

**Arrive early for the popular stations.** The Kempinski steak queue and the Conrad sashimi counter are the two places reviewers mention waiting. First seating is calmer than the second wave.

**Watch the calendar.** The Wanfu Hilton lunch deal ends December 27 and the Westin deal ends December 31, 2026; the two Xiamen buffets run into January and February 2027. If you are travelling over Christmas, confirm the blackout dates before you buy — December 24 is blacked out at the Kempinski, and New Year surcharges vary.

**Bring a translation app, not a phrasebook.** Menus at the stations are usually in Chinese with some English; pointing and the camera-translate function on your phone covers the rest. Staff at five-star hotel restaurants are used to foreign guests.
