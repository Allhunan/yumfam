**Nanjing Yihe Jiezi Garden Hotel （南京颐和芥子园酒店）** is a garden hotel with a real history: it stands on the site of the Jiezi Garden built 300 years ago by Li Yu, the Ming–Qing scholar who wrote *The Mustard Seed Garden Manual of Painting* — the classic Chinese painting textbook. The Yihe group restored the garden as a museum-style hotel, so you sleep inside a Ming–Qing garden of pavilions, rockeries and winding stone paths, right in the **Laomendong scenic area** — and the garden itself is open to the public by day but reserved for guests in the early morning and evening.

The hotel has just 63 rooms, all in a light-luxury Chinese style with wooden latticework, Sealy mattresses and calligraphy details (a framed first edition of the *Jiezi Garden Manual* hangs in the lobby, and the corridors are lined with antiques). The **¥2,240 package** covers 5 nights in a 30–41㎡ Yayun room — a king room of 35–41㎡ or a twin room of 30–34㎡ — with breakfast for two, **2 Jiezi Garden cultural exhibition tickets**, a welcome-fruit gift and one free minibar use, plus free parking. The 5 nights can be split across stays. Step outside and you are in Laomendong's lanes of snacks and shops; the Confucius Temple is about 1 km away, Wudingmen metro station about 620 m, and there is a private passage from the hotel straight into the garden.

Three more tiers run on the same base: **¥3,840** for 10 nights in the same 30–41㎡ room (was ¥6,400), **¥2,695** for 5 nights in a 44㎡ Yayun feature king room (was ¥3,850), and **¥4,620** for 10 nights in the 44㎡ room (was ¥7,700).
