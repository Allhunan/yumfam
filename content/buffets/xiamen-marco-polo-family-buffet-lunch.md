In Xiamen, "Marco Polo" is practically shorthand for one dessert: the **Napoleon cake**. Locals line up for a single slice on an ordinary day — with this deal, you can eat as many as you want. Twenty-plus years in the making, the Marco Polo buffet at **Xiamen Marco Polo Oriental Hotel** (Hubin North, Siming — Metro Line 2 straight to the door) still draws crowds for its **steamed seafood** (prawns, mussels, oysters, scallops, razor clams, tiny red snails — you pick, they steam), **sushi from the hotel's own Japanese restaurant** (将军日本餐厅 chefs on duty), and the **live grill stations** that made its name — "big slabs of meat, generous pours," in the buffet's own unapologetic words.

Beyond the headliners: a **noodle counter** (noodles, rice noodles, hofan with soup + toppings), **Chinese and Western hot dishes**, a full **dessert and fruit spread** (pudding, cakes, yogurt, mousse), and the café's **lakeside setting** with an open kitchen — you watch the chefs work while you eat, which doubles as peace of mind about what lands on your plate.
