**Deal price: 6 days / 5 nights from ¥3,080 per child** (≈$428, was ¥3,980, early-bird ¥900 off) · **Adults from ¥3,680** (≈$511) · Departures Dec 19, 2026 – Jan 20, 2027 · Confirm with customer service after booking

This is not a hotel deal — it is a 6-day guided snow journey into Inner Mongolia, built around one idea: the "fire" of the northern winter. The route runs from Beijing to the Chongli ski resort (a 2022 Winter Olympics venue), then deep into the snowfields of Ulan Butong and Keshiketeng. It is the only December-departure northern snow tour on the booking platform right now, and the pace is deliberately slow — fewer stops, more time in each place — which matters at minus twenty degrees with kids in tow.

The six days follow a "fire" theme the tour sets up for children: learning to ski, making an ice lantern, watching winter fishing, meeting the reindeer-herding Ewenki people, and ending in a private snow village. Highlights, as listed on the live product page:

- **Chongli 5S ski resort** — a Winter Olympics venue with a 2-hour ski lesson (1 coach per 5 children, nationally certified, CSIA/CASI system) and full gear included (helmet, boots, skis, poles). The group stays at a Winter Olympics designated hotel, so the slopes are on the doorstep.
- **Duolun Lake winter fishing** — a thousand-year-old fishing tradition with a heritage fishing master: the ceremony, breaking the ice, setting the net, and watching the catch come up through the ice. The lake's trapped air bubbles in the ice are part of the show too.
- **Reindeer tribe visit** — the Ewenki people, China's only reindeer-herding community: call the herd with a birch-bark whistle, try their handmade biscuits, and hear hunting stories in their forest home.
- **Off-road snowfield crossing** — hard-core SUVs (Toyota Prado / Tank 300) through the misty rime forest and open snowfields, plus a snow hike tracking animal footprints.
- **Private snow village** — a snow village open only to the group, with a village evening party, frozen pears and persimmons, and a market of local snacks.
